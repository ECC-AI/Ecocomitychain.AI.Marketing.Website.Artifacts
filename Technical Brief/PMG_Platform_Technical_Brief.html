EcocomityChain.AI · PMG Platform The Problem

Why existing tools are structurally insufficient

Risk lives where your tools stop looking

Every supply chain risk methodology in production today operates on supplier-visible data. The foundational academic work in supply chain risk quantification — the Ford-MIT Time-to-Recover model, Interfaces 45(5), 2015 — explicitly acknowledged that sub-tier data collection was deferred by design due to data availability constraints. That constraint was reasonable in 2015. The risks it leaves invisible are not acceptable today.

The material constraints that drive the longest disruptions — rare earth processing concentration, specialty alloy tooling lock-in, chemical synthesis bottlenecks — live at Level 4 through Level 7 of the bill of materials. A supplier's declared Time-to-Recover reflects their own operational capacity. It does not include the physics of the material chain beneath them.

The gap originates at Level 5: a sintered rare earth magnet production process with maximum manufacturing complexity, concentrated in a handful of facilities globally. The Tier 1 supplier's estimate is honest. It does not include what they cannot see beneath themselves. PMG was designed to close that gap.

Dual-Node Scoring

Both supplier and material nodes are scored independently in the same graph pass. A low-risk supplier can carry catastrophic material-level exposure three levels below them. Both scores are required.

4-Vector Risk Model

Manufacturing · Financial · Operational · Geopolitical. Each vector is scored per node. The weighted sum produces the node's overall risk score. Every weight is traceable through the XAI chain.

TTR Computed from Material Physics

Time-to-Recover is not self-reported. It is computed from the graph — tracing the actual constraint: tooling recut timelines, chemical re-qualification periods, geographic concentration at the ore source.

XAI Audit Trail — 100% Coverage

Every score carries a structured reasoning chain traceable to the computation that produced it, at the moment it was produced. Auditable by compliance teams. Ingestible by AI copilot systems.

EcocomityChain.AI · PMG Platform Prescriptive Intelligence

Beyond risk identification

From finding alternatives to understanding trade-offs

Standard prescriptive tools recommend an alternative supplier. ECC evaluates whether the alternative is viable — materially, structurally, and regulatorily — before the recommendation surfaces. The output brings procurement and engineering to the same table.

PATHWAY 01

Material substitution evaluation

When a material is at risk, the prescriptive layer queries a database of 570,000+ materials across 80+ international standards for global equivalents. Mechanical and physical properties of each candidate are compared against the performance specification. Compliance status against ELV, REACH, and RoHS is validated. Multi-criteria scoring ranks candidates by functional fit, supply concentration, and sustainability profile. Output: an engineering-grade substitution brief for procurement and engineering jointly.

PATHWAY 02

Sourcing viability simulation

Once a candidate material is identified, the prescriptive layer maps qualified manufacturers across a live supply graph of dependencies up to ten tiers deep — country-of-origin, capacity indicators, dependency strengths. "Does an alternative exist?" is answered alongside "Is this executable, by whom, and what does their dependency profile look like?" Simulation runs before the recommendation lands with the team.

PATHWAY 03

Cross-domain consequence analysis

A prescriptive action that reduces tariff exposure might change the DPP compliance baseline or introduce a new geographic concentration. Because the orchestration layer draws from the same material graph across all three domains — proactive, tariff, regulatory — every recommendation is evaluated for cross-domain consequences before it surfaces. The procurement lead and compliance lead see the same picture.

Resilience Fabric

Beyond the computable. The prescriptive layer also draws from a curated knowledge base built from hundreds of supply chain research papers and documented historical incidents — containing the mitigation strategies that were deployed, what worked, and what did not. When the platform identifies a critically important sub-tier supplier under financial stress, it surfaces options beyond replacement: supply chain finance instruments, inventory pre-build programmes, collaborative recovery frameworks. The right action is not always the obvious one.

Prescriptive intelligence is a cross-cutting layer available across all four intelligence domains. Partner-extended capabilities — material substitution intelligence and live supply graph simulation — are available through the ECC partner network. Partners available on request.

How PMG connects to your environment

Your stack stays intact. Your data stays in your infrastructure.

Integration model

PMG integrates with SAP and other SCM systems through published APIs or SAP's own agentic connectors. Existing procurement workflows, ERP configuration, and supplier relationships are untouched.

  • Read-only access. PMG does not write to your systems.
  • API-native. No direct database access. SAP ecosystem compatible.
  • Need-to-know scope. Integration covers only the data relevant to the scoped engagement.
  • Auditable. Every data access through the integration layer is logged.
  • Open signal ingestion. Architecture supports customer-owned assessment data as an additional input signal — scored alongside platform data without requiring code changes.

Deployment model

ECC deploys into your own Azure subscription and your own Neo4j instance. We provision and configure. You own the infrastructure.

  • Azure deployment in your tenancy. No shared cloud infrastructure operated by ECC.
  • Neo4j in your account. The material graph runs in an instance you own and control.
  • Data sovereignty. Your supply chain data never moves to ECC's environment.
  • Isolated per customer. No multi-tenant deployment model.

Security

Primary security mechanism is architectural: customer-owned infrastructure means supply chain data never moves to a shared environment. ECC does not hold, process, or store customer data outside the customer's own cloud accounts.

Security architecture documentation, threat model, network isolation design, data access controls, and testing results are available on request and prepared to enterprise standard. The ECC founding team includes a Microsoft-certified Cybersecurity and Solutions Architect.

DPP dual return

PMG builds its material graph using science-quality life cycle inventory data — CAS-level compound data, international LCI datasets, critical minerals sourcing intelligence. This is the same data foundation required for EU Digital Product Passport compliance. The data work ECC does for risk intelligence simultaneously builds the customer's DPP compliance baseline. One data investment. Two strategic returns.
