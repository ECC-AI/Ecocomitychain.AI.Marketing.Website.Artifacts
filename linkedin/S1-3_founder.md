I have the same conversation with CPOs and procurement heads every time.

I ask: how far down your supply chain can you actually see?

The answer is always some version of the same thing. Tier 1, with reasonable confidence. Tier 2, with effort. Below that — they rely on what their Tier 1 tells them.

Then I ask a different question: when your last significant supply disruption happened, where did it actually originate?

The pause that follows tells me everything.

It is almost never at Tier 1. A Tier 1 supplier failing outright is rare, and when it does happen it is usually visible in advance through financial monitoring or capacity signals. The disruptions that hit hardest — the ones that stop production lines for weeks, not days — almost always originate somewhere the organisation had no line of sight.

A fire at a semiconductor facility that held a process node nobody else could qualify for automotive in under twelve months. A government energy restriction in a single Chinese province that held 60% of a critical alloying element. A single raw material source that fed nineteen assemblies simultaneously — invisible from any Tier 1 risk register because nobody had traced the path that far.

The answer nobody has is not: which of my suppliers might fail. The answer nobody has is: what sits below the suppliers I can see, and what would happen to my business if it stopped.

That is the question we built ECC to answer.

Not to replace supplier risk management. To extend it to the depth where the actual risk lives.
