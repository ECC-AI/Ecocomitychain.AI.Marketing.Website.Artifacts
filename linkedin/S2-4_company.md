CBP collected **$195 billion** in customs duties in FY2025.

That is **250% more** than the year before.

This was not the result of more trade. Import volumes did not surge. The $195B is the result of higher effective rates on the same supply chains — IEEPA stacked on top of existing Section 301 and Section 232 regimes. The same BOM. The same suppliers. A materially higher liability on every entry carrying undocumented Chinese-origin content.

The enforcement activity that generated it: 71 focused assessments in March 2025 alone. $310M in previously uncollected revenue identified. $4.37M per assessment, on average.

Focused assessments do not arrive randomly. CBP's Automated Targeting System scores every entry against risk indicators — origin patterns, HS code classifications, declared values relative to known supply chain structures. When the system flags an importer, the assessment follows.

In parallel: 6,636 UFLPA shipments detained in H1 FY2025. 61% denied entry. Release rate: 6.5%.

Every company that had undocumented origin in their material stack in FY2024 has a larger undocumented liability in FY2025 — at the same import volumes, from the same suppliers, on the same BOM positions that were never traced.

The enforcement machine did not grow. The tariff stack did.
