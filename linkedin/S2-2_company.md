In FY2024, automotive accounted for **4%** of all UFLPA detentions.

In the first half of FY2025: **86%.**

No industry has moved that fast from peripheral to primary enforcement target — not even solar, which took two years to absorb what automotive absorbed in twelve months.

The trigger was a single Entity List addition in December 2023. One supplier. Four tiers deep. Components present across multiple vehicle lines at multiple OEMs simultaneously.

The financial stakes are different in automotive. A detained solar panel is a commodity. A detained vehicle — or a detention wave grounding an entire vehicle line pending component substitution — is a different category of financial event.

At a 61% denial rate, detention is not a cash flow timing problem. It is a revenue destruction event.

The solar industry spent two years building the supply chain documentation infrastructure that should have existed before enforcement began. Automotive is now at that same point.

The question is not whether your supply chain contains UFLPA-vulnerable components. At 86% of all detentions, the statistical answer is already clear.

The question is whether you know which BOM positions carry that exposure — before CBP does.
