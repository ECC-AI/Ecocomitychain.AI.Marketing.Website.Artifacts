In 2014, Ford partnered with MIT on one of the most rigorous supply chain risk studies in automotive history.

The goal: model supplier recovery times accurately enough to make better sourcing decisions.

The finding: the academic team acknowledged on page two that sub-tier data collection was deferred. The model was calibrated against Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers only — because data beyond that tier was not accessible.

That was a reasonable constraint in 2014.

It produced a specific, measurable gap.

A Tier 1 supplier declares recovery time based on their own operations. That estimate is honest. It does not include the chemistry of the material chain beneath them — the tooling constraints, the chemical re-qualification timelines, the processing geographies concentrated in a single region.

In one supply chain path we analysed, a Tier 1 declared eight weeks. The computed recovery time from the material chain beneath that supplier: fifty-two weeks.

Forty weeks of gap. Invisible to every tool that stops at Tier 2.

The Ford-MIT study gave us the framework. The gap it acknowledged gave us the problem to solve.
