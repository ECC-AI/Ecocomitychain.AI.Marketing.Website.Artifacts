Most AI risk platforms produce a score.

ECC produces a reasoning chain.

Every risk score the platform generates carries a structured explanation — traceable to the specific computation that produced it, at the moment it was produced. Not reconstructed after the fact. Not summarised from a model's internal state. A structured evidence chain that a compliance analyst can audit, a procurement engineer can interrogate, and an AI copilot system can read and act on.

This is not a reporting feature. It is infrastructure.

We published the first post in our Engineering Excellence series this week: how ECC threads structured explainability through a multi-engine risk computation pipeline — what we call the ExplanationContext pattern.

The argument in the post is that explainability built on top of a system after the fact is fundamentally weaker than explainability designed into the system's data model from the start. The former produces narratives. The latter produces evidence.

For supply chain risk intelligence, the difference matters. A score that cannot be explained is a score that cannot be challenged, validated, or acted on with confidence.
