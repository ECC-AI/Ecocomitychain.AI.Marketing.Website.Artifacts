February 2024. Thousands of Porsche, Audi, and Bentley vehicles impounded at US ports.

The cause: a component four supplier tiers deep.

In December 2023, CBP added Sichuan Jingweida Technology Group (JWD) to the UFLPA Entity List. JWD manufactures components used in automotive electrical systems. Those components had reached multiple global OEMs through a chain passing through a California-based distributor and a Tier 2 integrator.

VW Group had no system to identify which of its thousands of imported vehicle units contained JWD material — until CBP identified it for them at the port.

The Senate Finance Committee's May 2024 report documented what followed. The impoundments. The scramble. The three-month gap between when Lear notified BMW in January 2024 that JWD components were present, and when BMW stopped importing vehicles containing those components in April 2024.

The Senate committee called it a failure of self-policing.

It was not. It was a failure of information architecture.

A supplier-level flag creates a search problem. JWD was not a T1 or T2 supplier. It was a materials supplier to a component manufacturer that supplied a Tier 2 integrator that supplied the OEM. Standard compliance tools, working from the supplier relationship layer, cannot reach it.

The entity was on the list. The affected BOM positions were invisible.

That is the gap. Not intent. Not negligence. Architecture.
