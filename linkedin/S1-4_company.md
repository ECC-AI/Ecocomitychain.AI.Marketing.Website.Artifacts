Did you know most supply chain risk tools stop at Tier 2?

Not by design failure. By data architecture.

A supplier graph maps relationships between companies. Company A supplies to Company B. Company B supplies to Company C. The graph is as deep as the relationship data goes — and relationship data runs out around Tier 2 or Tier 3, where direct contracts and EDI connections fade into inferred connections and self-reported surveys.

The risks that caused the longest production stoppages in recent years did not originate at Tier 1 or Tier 2.

They originated at the material level. In the processing chemistry of a component. In the geographic concentration of a raw material source. In the tooling specificity of a manufacturing process that only a handful of facilities globally can run.

That information does not live in a supplier relationship. It lives in the material specification — in what a component is made of, where those materials come from, and what it would take to recover if that source disappeared.

ECC traces every assembly to its raw material source — eight levels deep. Not because depth is interesting. Because that is where the answer is.
