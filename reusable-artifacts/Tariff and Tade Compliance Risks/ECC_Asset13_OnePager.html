Automotive  ·  UFLPA Enforcement
Volkswagen Group
February 2024  ·  Porsche · Audi · Bentley
Thousands of Porsche, Audi, and Bentley vehicles were impounded at US ports. The cause: a LAN transformer component sourced from a Chinese manufacturer on the UFLPA Entity List — buried four supplier tiers deep, invisible to every compliance tool Volkswagen used. The violation was identified not by Volkswagen's internal systems, but by a supplier notification. CBP found what Volkswagen's tools could not see.
Vehicles impounded: Thousands of luxury vehicles across three marques
Revenue at risk: $80,000–$200,000+ MSRP per vehicle
Resolution timeline: 6+ weeks of delivery suspension
Root CauseZero visibility below Tier 2
BMW
May 2024  ·  Mini Cooper  ·  Senate Report
8,000 Mini Cooper vehicles imported with the same UFLPA-banned component — after BMW's Tier-2 supplier notified them directly in January 2024. BMW continued importing for three more months. The US Senate Finance Committee named BMW in a formal report. Senator Wyden called for CBP to "supercharge enforcement."
Vehicles affected: 8,000 — US-wide service action initiated
Regulatory outcome: Named in Senate Finance Committee report
Timeline: 3 months of continued imports post-notification
Root CauseNo system to act on the notification received
Consumer Goods  ·  Section 301 + IEEPA
Nike
FY2025  ·  CFO Disclosure  ·  Earnings Call
Nike's CFO confirmed on a June 2025 earnings call that US tariffs would add an estimated $1,000,000,000 in annual incremental cost. China accounted for approximately 16% of Nike's US footwear imports. The effective tariff stack on Chinese-origin goods reached 58.3%. Gross margins fell 140 basis points.
Nike had no BOM-level data to identify which specific product lines drove the highest tariff exposure, which materials within those lines caused it, or which sourcing switches would deliver the greatest saving per dollar of transition cost. The response was broad and reactive. The cost was not.
Annual tariff cost increase: $1,000,000,000 — confirmed by CFO
Gross margin impact: −140 basis points (Q3 2025)
Response: Price hikes + supplier exits + structural cost cuts
Root CauseNo material-level tariff attribution by SKU or BOM position

The Root Cause
Every incident on this page shares one cause. Standard supply chain risk platforms map supplier relationships. They show you who your suppliers are. They cannot show you what your suppliers' products contain — at the material level, the HS code level, the origin level.

The LAN transformer inside a German-assembled motor. The alloy powder inside a traction motor magnet. The tariff exposure buried four tiers deep in a product your customs entry classifies as German-origin. Standard platforms don't look inside. CBP does.
