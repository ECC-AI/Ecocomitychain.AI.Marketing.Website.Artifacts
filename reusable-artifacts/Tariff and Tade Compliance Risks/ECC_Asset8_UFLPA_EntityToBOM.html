What standard tools do
  • Entity screening at T1–T2 Match your supplier list against the UFLPA Entity List and flag the entity as non-compliant.
  • No component-level visibility No information on which product, which component, or which BOM position contains the entity's material.
  • Manual search burden Your compliance team searches thousands of components manually to find the affected part — if they can find it at all.
  • T3+ exposure is invisible If the entity is at T3 or deeper — as JWD was in the VW and BMW incidents — no standard tool finds it.
  • The result VW had thousands of vehicles impounded. BMW continued importing for three months after notification — not because they ignored the problem, but because they had no system to find which components to pull.
What ECC does
  • Material genealogy match — not supplier list ECC maps the UFLPA Entity List against the material genealogy graph at L3–L5. Not who your suppliers are — what your components contain.
  • Graph traversal on entity addition When JWD is added to the Entity List, ECC queries: which L3–L5 material nodes in the graph have this entity in their supplier relationship?
  • Complete affected assembly list Result: 47 component material nodes identified across 8 assemblies in the vehicle programme (illustrative — actual count depends on your BOM).
  • Per-node compliance action For each node: the specific assembly, the HS code, the annual import volume, and the resolution action — alternative supplier qualification or attestation — ranked by lead time.
  • Hours, not weeks The same analysis that would have taken BMW's compliance team weeks happens in a single graph traversal. Your team acts. Your vehicles ship.
