The tariff exposure in your supply chain
is not on your customs entry.
It is on your P&L.
Section 301, IEEPA, and Section 232 tariffs are assessed on materials at the point they cross the US border — not on the finished product. Untraced Chinese-origin content in imported components represents an unknown financial exposure that sits between your current cost base and your actual liability. ECC makes it visible, quantifiable, and resolvable.
The standard view: your vehicle is assembled in Germany or Tennessee. Your customs entry declares the correct origin. Your tariff rate is MFN. The problem: the tariff liability on Chinese-origin materials inside imported components is not determined by where the vehicle was assembled. It is determined by where the materials inside those components came from — at the chemical precursor level. Most supply chain platforms stop at Tier 1-2. Tariff exposure lives at L4-L5. The gap between those two layers is what this brief quantifies.
A
Scenario A
European-Assembled
Vehicle Import
Reference: BMW Group — ~200,000 vehicles/year imported to US from European plants. Source: BMW / US Dept. of Commerce, 2025.
Vehicle assembled in Germany, UK, or Austria. Customs entry filed as European-origin at MFN rate. Chinese-origin content at L4-L5 — magnets, steel, copper, electronics — is invisible on the customs entry. If CBP applies substantial transformation analysis and determines Chinese-origin content is dominant, retroactive reclassification is possible under 19 U.S.C. § 1592.
Risk Type
Latent CBP audit risk — contingent on focused assessment. Penalty on reclassification: up to 4× unpaid duties. Resolution path: attestation before audit begins.
B
Scenario B
US-Assembled Vehicle
with Imported Components
Reference: VW ID.4 Chattanooga, TN. Traction motor imported from VW Kassel, Germany (permanent magnet type). Battery cells from SK On, Commerce, GA. Source: VW Group Components / Electrive, 2022.
Vehicle assembled in the US. But the traction motor is imported from Germany and uses permanent magnets whose alloy powder origin is undocumented. The battery cells use NMC 811 cathode — over 90% of Korean cathode precursor was imported from China as of March 2025 (SNE Research). Each imported component with Chinese-origin content at L4-L5 carries existing tariff exposure today.
Risk Type
Existing exposure on imported component stream — not contingent on vehicle reclassification. Tariff liability accrues on each component crossing the US border with undocumented Chinese-origin content.
